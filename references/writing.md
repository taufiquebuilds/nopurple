# Writing the deck

Most AI slop is a writing problem. Fix the words and half the design problem disappears.

## The headline formula

A slide title is a full sentence that states the slide's point. Read only the titles in order, and they should tell the whole story.

**Formula:** what happened, plus the number or the "so what".

| Topic title (slop) | Point title |
| --- | --- |
| Client Retention | We kept 94% of clients this quarter, up from 81% |
| Market Overview | The market grows 18% a year, but three players hold 70% of it |
| Our Solution | One dashboard replaces the four tools your team switches between |
| Key Highlights | Revenue beat plan by 12%, driven by two enterprise deals |
| Next Steps | Approve one hire by 15 November to keep the Q1 launch date |
| Challenges | Onboarding takes 9 days, and 40% of trials quit before day 3 |

Rules:

- Use sentence case. Capitalise only the first word and names.
- Keep it to 15 words or fewer, and at most 2 lines on the slide. If it's longer, the slide holds two points: split it.
- Put the most important word or number early. Wrap the key number in `<em>` to colour it with the accent.
- Use a verb. "Q3 retention" is a label. "Retention rose to 94% in Q3" is a point.

## The storyline

Lead with the answer, then support it. Busy people decide in the first two slides.

A pattern that fits most business decks:

1. **Title:** the conclusion in plain words.
2. **The point, with its number:** what happened.
3. **Why:** the cause, as a chart or a comparison.
4. **So what:** the cost or the opportunity.
5. **The plan:** 3 steps.
6. **Proof:** a quote, a result or a customer.
7. **The ask:** the decision, the owner and the date.

For a pitch: problem, cost of the problem, solution, proof it works, why now, the business, the team, the ask.

For a talk: one surprising claim, why it's true, what to do about it, the one-line takeaway.

## Cut the words

- **Under 40 words per slide** is the default. A title plus one supporting line often does the job.
- Turn sentences into the number: "We saw a significant increase in conversions" becomes "Conversions: 2.1% → 3.4%".
- Move explanation, caveats and methodology into speaker notes.
- One idea per slide. If you write "and" in a title, consider two slides.

## Words to replace

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| Leverage, utilise | Use |
| Unlock, drive, elevate | Raise, grow, cut (say by how much) |
| Robust, seamless, best-in-class | Say what it does: "works offline", "set up in 5 minutes" |
| Significant, substantial, dramatic | The number |
| Journey | The process, the steps, or nothing |
| Synergies | What actually combines, and the result |
| Going forward | Next quarter, by March |
| Empower teams | Let teams (do what?) |

## Numbers

- Round for the slide (94%, $4.8k). Exact numbers go in notes.
- Always give the comparison: up from what, versus what, by when.
- Label units and time periods.
- Never invent a number, a quote or a customer. Use `[?]` and list it for the user.

## Before and after

**Before** (typical AI output):

> **Key Highlights** 🚀
> - 💡 Leveraged synergies to drive robust client engagement
> - 📈 Significant improvements in retention across all segments
> - 🤝 Strengthened relationships through proactive communication
> - ⚡ Implemented innovative monthly strategy sessions

**After** (two slides):

> **We kept 94% of clients this quarter, up from 81%.** Number layout: "+13 points since Q1".
>
> **The jump came the quarter the monthly calls started.** Bars layout: Q1 81%, Q2 87%, Q3 94%, with Q3 highlighted.
