# Choosing the chart

Start from the comparison the reader needs to make, not from the chart types a library offers.

## Ranking or comparing categories: horizontal bars

- Sort largest first, unless the categories have a natural order (age bands, months).
- Horizontal bars give long labels room without rotating them.
- Highlight the category the headline is about; keep the rest grey.
- Label each bar with its value at the end of the bar, and drop the value axis.
- For more than about 12 categories, show the top 10 and group the rest as "Other", or split the chart.

## Change over a few periods: vertical bars

- 2 to 12 periods. Time runs left to right.
- Start at zero, always. If the change is small relative to the total, show the change itself (for example "+3 points") as a bar or a number instead of truncating the axis.
- Highlight the period the headline talks about.

## A trend over many points: a line

- Use when there are more than about 12 points, or the shape matters more than single values.
- A line doesn't have to start at zero, but the axis range shouldn't exaggerate the change. Show a sensible range and label the start and end values.
- Label the latest value at the end of the line.
- Mark events on the line ("price change") with a short annotation rather than a legend.

## Several series over time: highlighted lines

- 2 to 4 lines at most. Label each line at its end instead of using a legend.
- Make the line the headline talks about accent-coloured and thicker. The others are grey and thinner.
- More than 4 series: use small multiples (one small chart per series, same scale) instead.

## Before and after for several items: slope chart

- Two columns (before and after) with a line per item. The steepest change stands out without any explanation.
- Label each end with the value, and the item's name on one side.
- Highlight the one or two items the headline is about.

## Share of a whole

- Ask first whether a chart is needed: "3 of 4 clients came from referrals" is clearer than a pie.
- A single horizontal stacked bar, with labelled segments, beats a pie for 3 to 5 parts.
- A pie or donut only for 2 or 3 parts, with no explosion, no shadow and no 3D, labelled directly.
- Never compare shares across several pies. Use bars.

## Distribution

- A histogram or a dot (strip) plot. Box plots confuse non-technical readers, so only use them for technical audiences.

## Two measures for the same items

- A scatter plot, with a few labelled points the headline mentions.
- Never use dual y-axes. They let any two trends look correlated. Use two charts side by side instead.

## Don't chart it

- One number: show it big, with its comparison ("94%, up from 81%").
- Two numbers: a sentence or a two-column comparison.
- A table of exact values people will look up: use a clean table, not a chart.
